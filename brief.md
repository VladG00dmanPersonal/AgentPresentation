# Presentation brief

## Topic

Soviet Constructivism in the 1920s–1930s.

## Context

History presentation.

## Audience

school

## Duration

Approximately 10 minutes.

## Expected size

Approximately 12 slides.

## Primary goal

Explain Constructivism not only as an architectural style,
but as a wider visual and cultural phenomenon.

The audience should understand:

- why Constructivism appeared;
- its relationship with the early Soviet period;
- its visual language;
- how its principles appeared in architecture;
- how similar ideas appeared in posters and graphic design;
- why the movement declined.

## Tone

Historical and analytical, but visually expressive.

This should not look like a generic school PowerPoint.

The visual design may reference Constructivist graphic language,
but should remain readable as a modern presentation.

## Design direction

Use Soviet Constructivism as inspiration.

Possible references:
- asymmetric compositions;
- strong geometry;
- photomontage;
- large typography;
- diagonal movement;
- constrained high-contrast palette;
- deliberate use of negative space.

The designer may reinterpret these principles.

Avoid:
- decorative Soviet clichés;
- hammer-and-sickle imagery unless historically relevant;
- making every slide red;
- fake distressed textures everywhere;
- overly theatrical propaganda aesthetics.

## User/designer relationship

The user controls:
- historical content;
- factual accuracy;
- overall narrative.

The agent controls:
- visual system;
- composition;
- typography;
- graphic language.

Important aesthetic decisions should be discussed rather than silently assumed.
